Overview

The lives of two of the most influential Christian authors in modern times were shaped decisively by war: Serving as second lieutenants in the British military, J.R.R. Tolkien and C.S. Lewis survived the mechanized slaughter of the First World War.

Scroll
Overview

Yet when they returned home, they discovered that the traditions, beliefs, and ideals central to Western civilization were under assault.

The First World War created a storm of disillusionment and cynicism about human freedom, democratic values, and traditional religion. A raft of ideologies rushed in to fill the void: communism, fascism, eugenics, scientism, modernism, and totalitarianism. The tragic result was a Second World War.

Scroll
Overview

Oxford University, where Tolkien and Lewis took up their post as instructors in English literature, was not immune to the crisis enveloping Europe. Both men were determined to fight back.

In the mid-1930s, as a “gathering storm” of evil descended upon the European continent, they rededicated themselves to their scholarly and literary pursuits. “Well, Tollers,” Lewis announced to Tolkien, “if they won’t write the kinds of books we want to read, we shall have to write them ourselves.”

Scroll
Our Crisis Moment

When our schools and universities no longer transmit the Judeo-Christian inheritance of our civilization, radical ideologies overtake our most important institutions. When the storytellers of our society—our filmmakers—embrace cynicism and relativism, they help create cynics and relativists. When a Meryl Streep has been cast as the male Christ-figure Aslan in Netflix's The Magician’s Nephew, we know our civilization is losing its moral bearings.

It was this attack on the moral foundations of the West that Tolkien and Lewis battled against. We have much to learn from their example.
